01

One chassis, two masterpieces一副底盘，两个杰作

Can a race car and a work of art share one foundation? The Type 57 already answered.

In 1937 and 1939, the Type 57 chassis won Le Mans outright in the streamlined 57G "Tank". The very same foundation underpinned the 57SC Atlantic — widely regarded as the most beautiful car ever built.

Destrier and Bolide continue that tradition: one chassis, two entirely different expressions. Above the windscreen, a hand-laser-engraved plate depicts both the 57G Tank and the Atlantic — the bond made permanent.

The third one-of-one of Programme Solitaire, after Brouillard and F.K.P. Hommage. Where its predecessors grew from emotive stories steeped in history, the Destrier works the other way around: no story to lean on — instinct and emotion, shaped directly.

02

One meter tall一米的宣言

What if Bugatti's most extraordinary engineering package were left untouched by wings, vents and aerodynamic addenda?

The answer: proportion.

Stripped of every aerodynamic appendage, the body is reduced to pure volume — just one meter tall, dramatically lower than any other Bugatti. The cabin crouches low between the front wings, poised like a warhorse at a standstill.

Destrier is named after the finest and strongest horse of the medieval knight: not the heaviest, not the fastest — the one that could be trusted.

03

The breathing C-line呼吸的 C 线

Traditionally an unbroken curve of brightwork — on the Destrier, it breathes.

Bugatti's C-line, traditionally an unbroken curve framing each side, now "breathes" on the Destrier.

It pauses just before the front wheel well, letting bare bodywork show through, then resumes its path to meet a horseshoe grille wider than the Bolide's. At the rear, an elegant blended wing echoes the Chiron Profilée, closing the silhouette with quiet grace.

04

Sapphire CelesteThe Colour

The blue was not chosen at random — the celebrated "Pope" Atlantic originally wore blue.

A bespoke, multi-layered colour created exclusively for this car and its owner.

"Sapphire Celeste" shifts with the light to reveal the full depth of the bodywork's volumes. Diamond-inspired sparkle flickers as light sweeps across; polished aluminum brightwork contrasts the refined hue.

An echo of history, in colour.

The Type 57SC Atlantic, chassis 57591 — the celebrated "Pope" Atlantic — originally wore blue, before being repainted black by its current owner. Destrier's blue is a quiet nod to that lineage.

Exposed carbon fiber is limited to the front splitter and rear diffuser, tinted to match.

05

A tailor's atelier裁缝的工坊

The cabin leaves the Bolide's motorsport cockpit far behind — three materials define it.

Every material was chosen the way a tailor selects cloth.

"Ambre Voyageur" leather and nubuck — a timeless warm brown — plus an advanced 3D-knitted textile: seamless, three-dimensional surfaces as technical as they are tactile. Copper yarns run through it as a circular "halo-line" around the cabin. The hammered brightwork returns at four touchpoints: door opener, air vents, steering wheel hub, entry plate.

06

The W16 beneath藏在车身下的 W16

A detail offered almost in passing: the Bolide's complete powertrain, unchanged.

Like the great Concours-winning cars of the past, Destrier's story is one of proportion and craftsmanship, not outright performance.

Beneath the bodywork sits the Bolide's complete W16 — 1,600 PS, the quad-turbocharged 8.0-litre engine at the peak of its development. The only part of the car left untouched: because it needed no changes.
